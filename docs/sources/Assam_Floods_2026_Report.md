## 1. How to read this file (important caveats)

- **"Recent flood" here means the July–August 2026 Upper Assam floods**, which began on 19 July 2026 and are the dominant 2026 flood event in the reports I found. I did not find a clearly separate earlier 2026 flood wave in my searches. One report dated 20 July already counted five flood deaths "this year" `[S21]`, so some deaths pre-date the main event.
- **Counts are snapshots, not cumulative totals.** "People affected", "villages inundated" and "crop area damaged" come from daily bulletins of the Assam State Disaster Management Authority (ASDMA) and change as water rises and recedes. A smaller number on a later date usually means water receded, not that damage was reversed.
- **Bulletin date vs publication date.** Reports often describe the previous night's bulletin. I use the date the source gives, and note offsets where visible.
- **Source quality varies.** Wire copy (PTI, ANI, IANS, AP) and established newspapers carry most of the numbers. A few items come from aggregator or AI-assisted sites; I mark those "lower confidence" in the source list and avoid leaning on them for headline figures.
- **Opinion/analysis is labelled.** The warning-system analysis in Section 3 comes from an opinion piece `[S24]`; treat it as analysis, not official record.
- **Latest information:** the most recent official update I found is dated **1 September 2026** `[S37]`. I found nothing newer on rehabilitation status. For anything after that, check ASDMA or current news.

## 3. What caused it

**Rainfall upstream.** On 19 July, heavy rain in Mon, Mokokchung and Wokha in Nagaland made the south-bank tributaries Dikhow, Disang and Dhansiri swell and flood Assam `[S1]`.

**The "cloudburst" dispute.**
- Assam's Chief Minister said the flood was caused by heavy rainfall and a cloudburst in Nagaland `[S1]`.
- The India Meteorological Department (IMD) rejected reports of cloudbursts and said prolonged heavy rainfall caused the Upper Assam floods `[S25]`.

**Analysis from an opinion piece `[S24]` (labelled as such):**
- An automatic rain gauge at Aboi in Mon district recorded 137 mm, accumulated over roughly 8–9 hours rather than one hour, so it did not meet IMD's cloudburst definition (100 mm in one hour).
- Three to four intense rain episodes in the preceding two weeks had saturated soil across the Mon hills.
- Charaideo recorded 170 mm, about 436% above its seasonal average.
- Water reached the Assam plains from the Naga hills in about 90 minutes.
- The piece reports that the Central Water Commission gauges, IMD's six-hourly Flash Flood Guidance System and the satellite-based FLEWS did not produce effective public warnings in that window, and that Nagaland authorities reportedly sent an alert to Assam's Chief Secretary within about 10 minutes, but no automated public alert reached downstream communities.
- It records a major breach in the Dorika River embankment.

**Residents' accounts `[S1]`:**
- Santak Panchali: water entered at about 1 pm and reached chest height within twenty minutes.
- Betbari village: everything submerged around 7:30 pm on 19 July; water from the Dikhow flowed into the Dorika, flooding the village; 99% of houses affected.
- Selengahat (Mariani, Jorhat): within 15 minutes of water reaching the courtyard it was chest-deep; 63 households lost all belongings.

**Assam government's position to the central team:** the floods in Charaideo, Sivasagar, Jorhat and Golaghat were unlike previous floods; Charaideo and Sivasagar are not traditionally flood-prone `[S30]`.

**Other factors raised (not official findings):** see [Section 10](#10-nagaland-side-causes-debated-and-criticism).

## 10. Nagaland side, causes debated, and criticism

**Nagaland.** 13 people died; Mon town was submerged and landslides/mudslides cut roads and electricity; the Nagaland government announced ₹4,00,000 ex gratia for the families of the dead and ₹74,000 for the injured; Naginimora (a border town less than 10 km from Bihubor) received flash flooding plus Dikhu river flooding, and the local Extra Assistant Commissioner reported giving immediate ration to 250 displaced households `[S1]`. NorthEast Now says nine deaths occurred in Mon during the single 24-hour event `[S24]`.

**Mining and land-use claims (positions, not findings):**
- Assam MLA Debabrata Saikia (Congress) called the flood a "meticulously engineered man-made disaster", blaming unchecked sand, stone and coal mining, and said he filed a PIL in the Gauhati High Court in 2019. The Naginimora EAC disputed the mining link, attributing the flood to torrential rain and upstream water `[S1]`.
- Researcher Mirza Zulfiqur Rahman pointed to wetland degradation, infrastructure projects hampering natural drainage, and unregulated mining in both Nagaland and Assam `[S1]`.

**Relief criticism.** A candidate from Mariani said government aid reached villages almost three days after the flood, while private, NGO and party aid came earlier `[S1]`.

**Political demand.** Assam Congress asked the PM to declare Assam's recurring floods and erosion a "national problem", citing that about 40% of Assam's area (31.05 lakh hectares) is flood-prone versus a national average of 10.2% `[S7]`.
